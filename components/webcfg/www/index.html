VFO-Knob ◀ Radio controls

 

This knob still uses the shipped password (admin/admin). Anyone on this network can key your transmitter with it — set your own below.

Radio

Up to four. A swipe up on the knob chooses another, and the knob restarts into it; here, In use takes effect when the knob next starts. Over the USB cable there is no choosing: the cable reaches one computer, or one radio.

The radio's own network user. On the IC-705: MENU » SET » WLAN Set » Remote Settings — Network Control ON, and a Network User with this name and password. On the IC-7610 and the IC-R8600: MENU » SET » Network — Network Control ON, and Network User1 with this name and password. The IC-R8600 is a receiver: the knob never keys it. The knob reaches the radio over WiFi, and applies changes here on its next boot.

The radio's IP address: the knob joins it as a MultiFlex station of its own, with its own slice, beside SmartSDR, AetherSDR or a Maestro. MultiFlex must be enabled on the radio. The knob reaches the radio over WiFi, and applies changes here on its next boot.

The knob talks to the wfview server built into the radio: SYSTEM SETTING » WFSERVER » START. Its user is user, password 123, unless changed there. The radio announces no name on the network, so give its IP address above. Changes here apply on the knob's next boot.

You are on the USB cable. Over it the knob finds AetherSDR on this computer by itself; this host is what it uses on WiFi. Open this page over WiFi and my IP fills it in.

The receiver's address as its page has it: https://name.tunnel.ubersdr.org through UberSDR's tunnel, or host:8080 on your own network. Its password is the receiver's bypass password, where its owner gave you one: it lifts the session time limit and the busy-receiver limits. Without one the knob listens as a guest, and when the receiver ends the session the dial asks before listening again. Changes here apply on the knob's next boot.

The reflector's name, as SvxLink knows it: its SRV record says which host and port to use (be.svx.link is reflector.be.svx.link:5300). The port counts only for a name without one. Changes here apply on the knob's next boot.

SmartLink

FlexRadio's service for a radio away from home, as SmartSDR and AetherSDR use it. The password goes to FlexRadio's login service and is not kept: the knob keeps the login it is given, until you log out here. Offered on the knob, these radios come after the ones above on the swipe up, marked SmartLink — the same radio can be there both ways. A radio reachable only by hole punching is listed, but not yet connected to.

Web SDRs

A second receiver beside the radio, following its frequency and mode: a KiwiSDR, a Web-888, or an UberSDR through its Kiwi input. On the knob, swipe down and turn to LOCAL or a receiver, then tap. With one playing, the radio is in the left ear and the SDR in the right, both brought to the same loudness; BALANCE, first on the swipe from the left, fades from one to the other. Its S-meter is the thin blue line outside the radio's, its reading the blue one under the radio's. The SDR is silent while you transmit.

Beside the UberSDR, a KiwiSDR or a Web-888 following it: swipe down on the knob and turn to LOCAL or a receiver, then tap. With one playing, the UberSDR is in the left ear and the KiwiSDR in the right, both brought to the same loudness; BALANCE, a swipe from the left, fades from one to the other.

The address is the receiver's own, with its port — host:8073 on most KiwiSDRs — not an https:// link through a proxy or a tunnel. The time-limit password, where a receiver has one, exempts you from its session limit. Saved with Save below.

Bluetooth headset

Lowering the boom — the headset's microphone live — transmits; raising it — muted — stops. Never by itself: when the headset connects or the knob starts with the boom down, the knob asks for it up first, in red, and only the next time it comes down does it transmit. Applies at once.

Put the headset in pairing mode, Find headsets, and tap it in the list. The knob remembers it, and calls it whenever it is switched on, as a phone does. While it is connected, the receiver's audio plays in it as well as on the jack.

While it is connected, the knob's audio plays in it as well as on the jack, its microphone is the one you transmit with — the knob's own is off — and its call button is the PTT: a press transmits, the next stops. The PTT slab on the knob shows the headset instead, and its microphone struck through in red while the headset has it muted; a tap there only stops a transmission. If the headset goes out of reach while you transmit, the knob stops transmitting.

Station

Leave both coordinates empty to publish no position.

Talkgroups

A + after a number raises its priority, up to +++: traffic there takes the knob off a quieter talkgroup. With both lists empty the dial steps through every talkgroup the reflector's portal names. The lock on the knob's face stops all switching.

Station and talkgroup changes apply when saved: the knob logs in again.

Certificate

The reflector knows a station by a certificate it signs. The request goes with the callsign and email above; the knob makes its key first, which takes up to a minute. Some reflectors sign at once, others wait for their sysop, and the knob keeps asking until the certificate arrives. It renews by itself.

Delete the key

The reflector ties the callsign to this key. With a new key the sysop has to remove the old certificate before signing again.

WiFi

Up to four networks — home, and a phone's hotspot, say. The knob joins whichever is in reach, the one it joined last first. With none of them in reach it puts up its own network, VFOKnob, as when it was new: join that with a phone to add one, and the knob goes on. Used when the knob is powered from a charger rather than a computer. Saved with Save below; tried when the knob next looks for a network, on the next boot at the latest.

Audio

Volume and mic gain take effect immediately.

Turning the knob, touching the screen or transmitting all count as use. Dimming leaves the frequency readable; going dark turns the backlight off.

Access

Guards this page only. It is sent unencrypted, so treat it as a lock on the door rather than a safe.

Firmware

There is one firmware per radio, and the knob runs one at a time. Switching installs the other radio's latest release.

The knob checks by itself on WiFi. Over the USB cable it has no route to the internet, so this page checks and does the downloading for it — same image, same signature check.
