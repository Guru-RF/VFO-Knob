VFO-Knob ◀ Radio controls

 

This knob still uses the shipped password (admin/admin). Anyone on this network can key your transmitter with it — set your own below.

Radio

Up to four. A swipe up on the knob chooses another, and the knob restarts into it; here, In use takes effect when the knob next starts. Over the USB cable there is no choosing: the cable reaches one computer, or one radio.

SmartLink

FlexRadio's service for a radio away from home, as SmartSDR and AetherSDR use it. The password goes to FlexRadio's login service and is not kept: the knob keeps the login it is given, until you log out here. Offered on the knob, these radios come after the ones above on the swipe up, marked SmartLink — the same radio can be there both ways. A radio reachable only by hole punching is listed, but not yet connected to.

Web SDRs

A KiwiSDR or a Web-888 lists who listens, for its owner: the knob goes by this name there — your callsign, say. Left empty, VFO-Knob. Saved with Save, and told at once to a receiver playing.

A second receiver beside the radio, following its frequency and mode: a KiwiSDR, a Web-888, or an UberSDR through its Kiwi input. On the knob, swipe down and turn to LOCAL or a receiver, then tap. With one playing, the radio is in the left ear and the SDR in the right, both brought to the same loudness; BALANCE, first on the swipe from the left, fades from one to the other. Its S-meter is the thin blue line outside the radio's, its reading the blue one under the radio's. The SDR is silent while you transmit.

Each owner's limits are kept. A receiver that turns the knob away because its listening time for the day is used up (day limit) is left alone, through restarts, until its own status shows it has restarted — or, on a KiwiSDR, a day has passed. Choosing it again is one more try, and the knob takes two such refusals from a receiver at most: a Kiwi bars an address for good after five. A receiver with time limits that leaves a login unanswered counts as one too: its answer may have been such a refusal. One that ended the session for idling (time up) or was sent away by its owner (kicked) is not asked again until you say so: the knob asks LISTEN AGAIN. One that refused the knob, or its password, or lets no apps listen, waits to be chosen again; one that is full is asked again every minute or two. The knob tries a receiver at most six times in ten minutes on its own; after a crash, what held a receiver back still holds it, and after a run of crashes the knob waits before it tries at all. With its settings memory full (MEMORY FULL) it logs in to a receiver with time limits only when you choose it.

Bluetooth headset or speaker

The knob tells a speaker from a headset by itself — by its class and the services it lists, or by its hanging up a call's audio at once — and remembers it. Use as changes it.

Level sets how loud the knob sends this speaker or headset its audio, on top of the VOLUME, from 24 dB less to 12 dB more in steps of 3 dB: heard at once, and kept for it. Until one is set a headset gets the jack's level, and a speaker 12 dB less — a loudspeaker fills a room with what suits earphones. Above 0 dB a loud passage that would pass full scale is turned down to it smoothly, never clipped.

Lowering the boom — the headset's microphone live — transmits; raising it — muted — stops. Never by itself: when the headset connects or the knob starts with the boom down, the knob asks for it up first, in red, and only the next time it comes down does it transmit. Applies at once.

Put the headset or speaker in pairing mode, Find headsets, and tap it in the list. The knob remembers it, and calls it whenever it is switched on, as a phone does. While it is connected, the receiver's audio plays in it as well as on the jack. A speaker plays the receiver the same way, a fifth of a second or so behind the jack. One that takes its volume from what plays to it — most do — turns itself to the knob's VOLUME, and its own buttons turn the knob's; one that does not keeps its own, and the knob turns down what it sends.

Station

Leave both coordinates empty to publish no position.

Talkgroups

A + after a number raises its priority, up to +++: traffic there takes the knob off a quieter talkgroup. With both lists empty the dial steps through every talkgroup the reflector's portal names. The lock on the knob's face stops all switching.

Station and talkgroup changes apply when saved: the knob logs in again.

Telephone

One SIP account, over UDP: the knob registers as soon as it is saved, and finds its way out through your router by itself.

Call

For other programs, behind this page's login: GET /api/phone says what the telephone is doing, GET /api/phone/history its recent calls; /api/phone/dial?number=…, /api/phone/answer, /api/phone/hangup and /api/phone/dtmf?digits=… act.

Favourites

The dial steps through them, and the slab calls the one shown.

Google Contacts

Your starred contacts become the favourites, every number each, and are synced again every six hours; a sync replaces the list above. The client is your own, from a Google Cloud project: see GOOGLE.md for its five steps, and what each message here means.

Certificate

The reflector knows a station by a certificate it signs. The request goes with the callsign and email above; the knob makes its key first, which takes up to a minute. Some reflectors sign at once, others wait for their sysop, and the knob keeps asking until the certificate arrives. It renews by itself.

Delete the key

The reflector ties the callsign to this key. With a new key the sysop has to remove the old certificate before signing again.

WiFi

Up to four networks — home, and a phone's hotspot, say. The knob joins whichever is in reach, the one it joined last first. With none of them in reach it puts up its own network, VFOKnob, as when it was new: join that with a phone to add one, and the knob goes on. Used when the knob is powered from a charger rather than a computer. Saved with Save below; tried when the knob next looks for a network, on the next boot at the latest.

Audio

Volume and mic gain take effect immediately. The knob's own microphone and a Bluetooth headset's each have their gain: the dial turns the one in use.

The volume takes effect immediately. A receiver's firmware has no microphone to set.

Turning the knob, touching the screen or transmitting all count as use. Dimming leaves the frequency readable; going dark turns the backlight off.

Turning the knob, touching the screen or a call, from its first ring to its end, all count as use: a call ringing in lights a screen that had dimmed or gone dark. Dimming leaves the screen readable; going dark turns the backlight off.

Access

Guards this page only. It is sent unencrypted, so treat it as a lock on the door rather than a safe.

Firmware

There is one firmware per radio, and the knob runs one at a time. Switching installs the other radio's latest release.

The knob checks by itself on WiFi. Over the USB cable it has no route to the internet, so this page checks and does the downloading for it — same image, same signature check.
