# The Elecraft K4 firmware

`vfo-knob-k4`, a **beta**, makes the knob a remote client of an
[Elecraft K4](https://elecraft.com/products/k4-direct-sampling-sdr-transceiver):
the dial tunes it, the slab keys it, the S-meter and the transmit meters show
what the radio measures, and its receive audio comes out of the jack while
the knob's own microphone goes back — over WiFi, with no computer in between.
It speaks what K4remote and the K4/0 speak: the radio's remote server. Its
face is black with white digits, gold accents and green meters (colours of
our own choosing, not the radio's).

**Tried on one K4** (2026-11-07, DC0SK's): it logs in, the dial, the meters and
PTT work, receive audio is clean, and an over goes out at a sensible ALC level
(see Audio). It was written from Elecraft's Programmer's Reference (rev. D12)
and K4remote's notes on the remote protocol, and is also run against a mock K4
(`tools/mock_k4.py`) with the knob's own code on a PC (`tools/k4host`) -- the
log-in, the dial, the editors, an over, a tune carrier, a refused key, a key
the radio ignores or takes late, a wrong password and a radio that falls
silent. What another radio does differently is what the next knob will find;
please report it, with the knob's log (**Report a problem** on its
configuration page). There are no pictures of its screens yet: they are the other
firmwares' (see [the AetherSDR guide](aethersdr.md)) in the K4's colours.

## Set the radio up

The knob is a *client* of the K4's server, as K4remote is. On the radio:

1. Software R36.BETA1 or later (earlier ones have no remote server).
2. Tap the **globe** button, then **SERVER SETTINGS**.
3. Give it a **Password**. Letters and digits; **not `$`**; it is case-sensitive,
   and the knob keeps up to 32 characters of it.
4. **Max Clients** 1 or more. With a client already connected — a K4remote, a
   K4/0 — the knob is a second one, and every client sees the others' changes:
   a tune at the knob moves the other's dial.

Then on the knob's configuration page: the radio's **IP address** (the K4 does
not announce a name), its **port**, and the **password**. A knob reaches the
radio over WiFi; the USB cable has no route to it.

**The port.** Elecraft's manual documents port 9204 and says nothing of
another. The knob speaks the *clear* port, 9205, which K4remote's notes give,
and does not speak 9204, the encrypted one (TLS with a pre-shared key). On
9205 the password travels as its SHA-384 hash, and everything else — the
frequency, the audio — in the clear. Keep it to a network you trust, and never
forward it over the internet; reach a K4 away from home through a VPN to its
network. If your K4 does not answer on 9205, say so in a bug report, with
what it does answer on.

## What the knob does

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tune** | VFO A: the dial, the step by digit, the acceleration — as everywhere. A change at the radio's front panel, or by another client, moves the dial once it has rested. 100 kHz to 54 MHz. |
| **Mode** | USB, LSB, CW, CW-R, AM, FM, and DIGU and DIGL for the K4's DATA and DATA-R. |
| **Filter** | The passband's width, as the K4 keeps it (its BW, with the centre pitch IS it already has). |
| **AGC** | Fast, slow or off. |
| **RF gain** | Beside the S-meter, 0 to −60 dB in steps of 5: the K4's RG, an attenuation. |
| **RIT** | An offset of up to ±9.999 kHz; RIT is on while it has one. |
| **DSP** | Swipe from the right: **NB**, **NR**, **ANF** and, in CW, **APF**, each lit when on. NR comes on at the level the radio already has it at; APF at its width. |
| **Meters** | The S-meter (the radio's high-resolution SMH, asked for ten times a second); in transmit, forward power in watts — the K4 reports tenths of a watt below its high-power range, and the knob reads them so — SWR, and the microphone's level. |
| **PTT** | Tap to key, tap to unkey, as on the other firmwares: the radio's `TX;` and `RX;`. A key is confirmed by the radio saying it transmits, and refused, with its reason on the face, when it says no (an error such as *TX inhibited*). A radio that stops answering for 6 s while keyed is dropped. |
| **Tune carrier** | Swipe down, **TUNE**: the radio's own tune (`TU1;`), never longer than 30 s. |

What is not here: the sub receiver, VFO B and split, the memories, the
antenna tuner and the antenna choice, the panadapter (it is never asked for),
CW from a paddle or the keyer, and the radio's own power setting.

## Audio

Opus both ways at 12 kHz — the radio's own remote audio: stereo from the
radio (Main on the left, Sub on the right), mono to it, 20 ms a frame. The
knob plays **Main**, in both ears; the sub receiver is not played. What the
radio sends is doubled up to the knob's 24 kHz, and the microphone is halved
to 12 kHz — by the mean of each pair of samples, which is enough for voice
and not for anything past 3 kHz. A lost frame is concealed, up to three in a
row. In an over the radio's own audio is not played: the encoder wants the
time.

What a real K4 taught:

- The radio sends **480 samples a channel** (40 ms) in packets of 900-1150
  bytes, about 25 a second; the decoder runs on core 1, away from the network.
- The radio's remote audio input has **no gain of its own**: its MIC setting
  does nothing to it, with the microphone unplugged either. A peak of -23 dBFS
  already read 7 on its ALC, whose bar ends at 8. So the knob turns the
  microphone down by 20 dB for the K4, with the TX slider on top, and mutes
  the first 300 ms of each over, where the microphone's start-up pop would
  otherwise hit the ALC.
- The radio takes **about a second** from `TX;` to transmitting, and does not
  key at all while no audio is streaming. A key it has not taken after 1.5 s
  is called refused, unkeyed with `RX;`, and ends there: only an answer to a
  `TQ;` asked with that `RX;` counts as proof of receive, a stale status does
  not.

## If the knob says NO LINK

- A weak or lossy WiFi link looks like everything below. At -75 dBm and worse
  the knob's sends stall for seconds, the radio seems to go silent, and a key
  that cannot be confirmed ends in the knob **restarting**, deliberately, so
  that nothing is left transmitting. Keep the knob in the clear (not in the
  hand, not behind the cable) and check with `ping -c 60 -i 0.2 <knob>`: a good
  link loses nothing and answers in a few ms.
- **NO ANSWER** — the radio's address is wrong or it is off, or its server is
  off (Max Clients 0), or it listens on another port than the one on the
  page.
- **PASSWORD?** — the radio hung up on the knob within three seconds, before
  saying anything: what it does on a wrong password. A connection that dies
  later is the link (NO LINK), not the password. The password is case-sensitive; `$` is not allowed
  in one. The knob keeps trying, and the message stays until it is in.
- It connects, and then the face shows nothing: the radio answered, but not
  with its state. Report it with the log.

## Building and checking

```sh
idf.py -B build_k4 -D VFO_RADIO=k4 build       # the firmware (the first build writes dependencies.k4.lock)
RADIO=k4 tools/release.sh <version>            # a release
cmake -B build_host -S test/host && cmake --build build_host --target test_k4_parse
build_host/test_k4_parse                       # the protocol layer: framing, audio, CAT text, fuzzed
make -C tools/k4host test                      # the client against the mock K4, seven scenarios
```
